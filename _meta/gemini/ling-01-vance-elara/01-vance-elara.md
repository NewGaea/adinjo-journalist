# Field Linguist's Log: Entry #1

## Observation

I have received, written in Latin script (they appear much further along in understanding us than we are them!) two short phrases:

1. _Trininu shoxaton._
2. _Trininu shoxatol._

## Immediate Analysis

- **Lexicon:** There appears to be a stable root or word, `Trininu`.
- **Morphology:** The second element, `shoxato-`, exhibits variation in its final segment. We have a suffix or ending alternation between `-n` and `-l`.

## Hypotheses

- **Syntax:** Given the repetition, `Trininu` is likely the subject or the topic of the utterance.
- **Inflection:** The shift from `-n` to `-l` represents a change in grammatical category. This could be:
    - _if `shoxato` is Verbal:_ A change in person ("I eat" vs. "He eats"), tense ("eats" vs. "ate"), or mood.
    - _if `shoxato` is Nominal/Adjectival:_ A change in gender, number, or case.

## Request for Confirmation

To proceed, I need the English translation of these two phrases. This will allow me to identify what grammatical function the `-n` vs. `-l` distinction serves.
