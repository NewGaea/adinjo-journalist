## **Analysis:**

1. **Lexicon (`shoxa-`):**
    - Clue: Produces sound, but not speech.
    - **Hypothesis:** **"To sing"** (or perhaps "to shout/call"). "Sing" fits well with high-culture or ritualistic contexts often found in conlang tropes.
2. **Morphology (Noun `Trini` vs. `Trininu`):**
    - The verb form (`shoxaton`/`shoxatol`) remains identical despite the subject changing from `Trininu` to `Trini`.
    - This implies the verb **does not conjugate for number**, or at least not for this specific distinction.
    - **Hypothesis:** `Trini` is the singular root. `-nu` is a **Plural** suffix.
    - _Stress implication:_ If `Trininu` is /triˈni.nu/ (stressed on the _ni_), I am going to guess that `Trini` is **/ˈtri.ni/**, suggesting that stress might stay on the root syllable, or follows a penultimate pattern that shifts with the suffix.
3. **Morphology (Verb Suffix `-tol`):**
    - Eliminated: Tense (Past/Future), Polarity (Negative).
    - Context: We have Simple Present (`-ton`).
    - **Hypothesis:** This is an **Aspect** distinction.
    - If `-ton` is Simple/Habitual ("sings"), I posit `-tol` is **Continuous/Progressive** ("is singing").
