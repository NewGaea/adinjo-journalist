## Grammar Update

- **Lexicon:** `shoxa-` = "to sing".
- **Noun Morphology:**
    - `Trini` = Singular (Unstressed, or so I assume for now).
    - `Trininu` = Plural (Stressed on penultimate: /tri**'ni**.nu/).
- **Phonology Note:** The existence of completely unstressed words suggests a stress system likely dependent on syllable weight, moraic count, or affixation. I also recall we have eliminated the possibility it is consistently penultimate.

## Analysis of `-tol`

Grace asked what possibilities remain if Tense and Aspect are ruled out. If `shoxaton` is "Trini sings" (Indicative/Statement), and `shoxatol` contrasts with it:

1. **Mood/Modality:** The speaker's attitude toward the action.
    - _Imperative:_ "Trini, sing!" (Command)
    - _Subjunctive/Optative:_ "Trini may sing" / "If Trini sings" / "Would that Trini sing."
    - _Deontic:_ "Trini must sing" / "Trini should sing."
2. **Sentence Type (Illocutionary Force):**
    - _Interrogative:_ "Does Trini sing?"
3. **Evidentiality:** The source of the information.
    - _Non-visual/Hearsay:_ "Trini sings (so I hear/am told)."
4. **Agreement (Gender/Class):**
    - If the verb agrees with the subject's gender, `-tol` could indicate a different gender than `-ton`. (e.g., "Trini [masc] sings" vs "Trini [fem] sings").

### **Hypothesis 6**

Given the minimal pair and the fundamental nature of early language learning texts, I am going to guess that this is a distinction of **Sentence Type**.

**Proposed Gloss:**

> Trini sing-Q
>
