## First Thoughts

This is a true puzzle. I have systematically eliminated the following options:

- **Tense** (Past, Future)
- **Aspect** (Continuous)
- **Mood** (Imperative, Interrogative)
- **Polarity** (Negation)
- **Agreement** (Person, Number, Gender)

When the standard "TAM" (Tense-Aspect-Mood) and Agreement markers are removed, we are left with categories that alter the relationship between the verb and its arguments (Voice/Valency) or change the nature of the word itself.

### Hypothesis 7: Voice (Passive)

In many natural languages (like Classical Latin), Voice is a primary conjugation category distinct from Tense/Aspect.

- Active: The subject performs the action.
- Passive: The subject receives the action.

If `shoxa-` means "to sing":

- `Trini shoxa-ton` = "Trini sings."
- `Trini shoxa-tol` = **"Trini is sung."** (i.e., Trini is the subject of the song; someone is singing about Trini).

### Hypothesis 8: Valency (Causative)

A morphological change that adds an argument.

- `Trini shoxa-tol` = **"Trini makes (someone) sing."**

### Hypothesis 9: Nominalization (Agentive)

Perhaps `shoxatol` isn't functioning as a finite verb here, but as a predicate noun.

- `Trini shoxa-tol` = **"Trini is a singer."**

## Selection

I am leaning heavily toward Voice as the strongest candidate. Hypothesis 8 seems weak as a Causative without any additional parameters seems to be a stretch. Hypothesis 9 seems viable, but less so if it is truly a parallel structure to the present tense. The Passive makes more sense, "Trini is sung about."

### **Proposed Gloss**

> **Trini shoxa-tol**
> 
> Trini sing-PASS
> 
> "_Trini is sung (about)._"
