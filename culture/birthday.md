# Birthdays & Namedays in Adin Culture

The Adin observe both the day when someone was born and the day they were named, which in modern culture is often 7-10 days after birth.[^1] The former is usually a small observance between child and parents, perhaps including siblings living at home, called the [_workajia_](lexicon/w/workajia), which become less frequent during adulthood. The latter is usually a larger celebration including friends, extended family, and others in the community, called the [_menajia_](lexicon/m/menajia) which usually includes gift-giving.

## Workajia

Workajia is generally celebrated through childhood as a time for parents to recognize the child for their actions and character growth over the previous year. There are seldom gifts exchanged other than affection or tokens thereof, such as a nice personal meal, or a personally made tokem of familial affection.

Workajia are celebrated less often in adulthood, but when they are, they are seen as a way for the grown child to give back to their parents in a show of appreciation for the hard work that went into raising them.

## History of Separation
### Superstition

In the past, it was believe that magic-users and astrologers who knew both the name and birthdate of a person could use this information to manipulate fate and compose more powerful curses and hexes, so the date of birth and the ceremony of naming were placed on separate days in order to subvert this belief. While it has been proven that this is not true, and the time between the two dates has closed as a result, the practice of delaying naming remains common within Adin cultures.

### Infant Mortality

Another reason for the delay between birth and naming is that infant mortality was once higher than it in modern times. The higher the rate of infant mortality is in a generation, the more likely parents are to delay naming their newborns until they are confident that they will survive to childhood.
