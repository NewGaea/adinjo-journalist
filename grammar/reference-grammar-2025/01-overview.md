# 1: The Adinjo Journalist Language and People

Adinjo Xoltwatax, translated into English as Adinjo Journalist, and sometimes called either Adinjo or Journalist, is a standardized and well preserved form of the Adinjo languages spoken around 2500 years ago on Gaea. There are three known extant dialect families of Adinjo Journalist, and this grammar focuses specifically on the Olympian dialect family, most particularly on Neo-Olympian Adinjo Journalist. 

## 1.1: Classification of Journalist Dialects

The three primary extant dialect families of Adinjo Journalist are referred to as the Olympian, Valhallan, and Artexan families. These are based on the colony ships which left Gaea in the last 2000 years or so. At present, the Olympian dialect family is treated as the standard, as the language has returned to Gaea with the Neo-Olympian diplomatic mission.

The Olympian family of Adinjo Journalist dialects is the most widespread dialect family on Gaea, Atrusius, and on the imperial moon Gurion above Atrusius. More than 20,000 members of the Diplomatic Mission on and around Gaea speak Neo-Olympian Journalist, and more than 4,000 members of the mission (and their offspring) living on Gaea speak new dialects which have been arising in the last 25 years.

## 1.2: Establishment of a Formal Structure

Journalist is itself a formalized version of earlier Adinjo languages which was established for the purpose of diplomacy with the other humans of the mid-first-millenium BCE. It does include borrowings from significant languages around the Mediterranean, from both the Afroasiatic and Indoeuropean families.

As the Adin moved to spaceships, preparing to move out into the space beyond Gaea and Sol, the emperor Kronos instituted a fixed form of the language to be used for interstellar communications, to be kept in archived form on each of the ships sent out.

While it is not mandatory, the preservation of Journalist has led to many scientific, thaumic, and various popular artistic works being produced in Principal Journalist and translated into other dialects and languages from it. While all members of the Adin Diplomatic Mission are trained in their shipboard standard, those living outside of Diplomatic ships often use far more relaxed and casual forms of the language, and those raised off ship may struggle with some of the formalities of the formal language.
