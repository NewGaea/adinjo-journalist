# 2: Phonology

## 2.1: Vowel System

There are 10 vowel phonemes in Journalist. Each vowel has a paired partner which is considered either tense or lax. In the diagram, paired vowels show the tense vowels on the left, lax on the right. Both central vowels are lax.

|       | front | central | back |
| ----- | ----- | ------- | ---- |
| close | i, ɪ  |         | u    |
| mid   | e, ɛ  | ʌ       | o, ɔ |
| open  | æ     | a       |      |

In traditional Adinjo material, tense vowels may be marked with an _asènt dai_ or acute accent, while lax vowels may be marked with a _blut dai_ or grave accent. In normal orthography these marks also control word stress, so this grammar will use the circumflex to mark tense and the breve to mark lax when it is otherwise following natural stress.

In addition to the above, leading post-consonantal \<o> and \<i> may trigger labialization and palatalization (respectively) if they are part of a diphthong, while \<e> in such positions will often cause the following vowel to fricativize into a consonant.

Vowels generally form diphthongs when they come in contact with each other. When they do not, the Adin convention is to separate them with an apsotrophe.

## 2.3: Stress

While stress can be explicitly marked in Adinjo, this is considered _unnatural_ stress. Natural Stress can be calculated in Adinjo by using archiphonemes //I, U, E, O//, only in words which have no _tense_ vowel marks.

By default, unnatural stress take precedence over natural stress. Thus, if you see any _asent dai_ or acute accents in a word, the earliest one takes primary stress and any later *asentu* take secondary.
### 2.3.1: Primary Stress

In words with natural stress, you must scan the word for the earliest occurrence of the closest and frontmost vowels. For this purpose, //I// represents both \<i, ì>, with each other archiphonemic vowel representing its lowercase forms. If there is no //I//, proceed through the list seeking //U, E, O// in that order. Note that this includes diphthongs, which are ranked as the closest, frontest vowel in the diphthong.

For example, in the word _adin_ 'everybody' the primary stress falls on the second syllable:  /a.dîn/. In _niejin_ 'prayer' the primary stress falls on the second //I// or the second syllable: /ɲe.ʒîn/.

- _ayginsten_ 'playing a game' ${\rightarrow}$ /ai.gîn.sten/

Primary stress adds length and increases the tenseness of the vowel receiving it.

### 2.3.2: Secondary Stress

Secondary stress is metrical, based on the position of primary stress. After finding the position of primary stress, every other syllable before and after it will have secondary stress. Secondary stress increases the tension of the vowel, but does not lengthen the syllable.

- _blani'urdin_ 'cat-like person' ${\rightarrow}$ /bla.nî.ur.dĭn/
- _galmcabind_ 'mood showing' ${\rightarrow}$ /gălm.tsa.bînd/

Because of this pattern, naturally stressed words can always be classed as **iambic** or **trochaic**.
