# 4: Verbal Morphology

Adinjo Journalist has two primary verbal paradigms, the _votenu tardai_ "t-form verbs" and _votenu kundai_ "kun-form verbs." In English, we refer to them as "ton verbs" and "kun verbs," respectively. It is widely held that the _kun verbs_ are older and more likely to descend entirely from older Atlantean verbal roots, and they are thought of as being less regular than the _ton verbs._ These two paradigms do have different dictionary forms.

In both forms, however, there is some room for a verbal modification complex which allows certain common modifiers to become incorporated into the verbal structure.

## 4.1: All Verbs

Verbs represent actions, and may incorporate nouns to make more specific, or sometimes unique, words. For example, the word _aye_ "hand" can be incorporated with _qon.ij_ "writing" (a ton verb) to create _yeqonij_ "writing by hand." We will describe this in more detail later, but it is possible to break down many longer verbs and guess at their meaning even if one is not already familiar with the composition itself.

All verbs in Journalist have three tenses, the past, present, and future. Ton verbs have five voices -- Root or Infinitive, Gerund, Negative, Active (which is unmarked), and Passive (which is marked), while Kun verbs have only four (they lack the distinction of Passive voice), and all conjugation for tense and voice happen following specific rules according to the verbal paradigm.

The verbal complex can be suffixed onto any conjugated verb, allowing a variety of aspects, moods, and adverbial elements to be attached, indicating things such as permission, ability, quality, and relative comparators. For example, _ic_ "to be" (a kun verb in present tense) can be conjugated to _içrin_ "to be well" or "to be in a positive state."

### 4.1.1 TON Verbs

Ton verbs, officially referred to as _votenu taru_ or _votenu tardai_, are sometimes unofficially referred to as _votenu kijuçi_ or "regular verbs," because their conjugations are always suffixed or applied almost entirely to the end of the root, but they have a system of principal parts or forms which must typically be learned separate from the rules of the paradigm.

For ton verbs, the principal parts are the _infinitive_, the _gerund_, and sometimes the _nominal_. These are usually taught and learned together, in the form "infinitive, gerund, nominal," such as learning the verb **_julax_** "to change" as "_julax, julaxen, julaxan._" For verbs without a separate nominal form, the gerund is also the nominal.

While the gerund form ending of ton verbs can vary wildly, it never changes the infinitive root, it is always a suffix (or, very rarely, a null). Because of this, ton verbs are listed in dictionaries with a full stop dot dividing the infinitive root and the gerund suffix (if any) -- for example *açoré.ru* "unfolding" -- and is defined or glossed in its gerund form.
