# Pokémon Names in Generation 1

## 001. Bulbasaur

Originally named フシギダネ, meaning "mysterious seed," and punning of the phrase, 不思議だね, "Strange, isn't it?". The English name refers to "bulb" as a type of seed, and "saur" as in dinosaur. Similarly the name sounds vaguely like "bulbous" and "bizarre."

- _Ire_ is the Adinjo Journalist word for seed.
- _Rukib_ is a term for something odd or unusual or uncommon.
- _Náj_ refers to frogs.

**_Rukirnáj_** is proposed meaning "Strange-seed frog."

## 002. Ivysaur

Originally named フシギソウ , meaning "mysterious grass," and punning off the phrase 不思議そう, "It's certainly strange." The English name refers to its growing plant as "ivy" a type of vine, while retaining the "saur" element of Bulbasaur.

- _Acoré_ and _reixa_ can both mean "to bloom."
- _Rukib_ and _náj_ can be preserved from **_Rukirnáj_** "Bulbasaur."

**_Rukréináj_** is proposed with the meaning "Strange-bloom frog."

## 003. Venusaur

Originally named フシギバナ, meaning "mysterious flower." It is unclear whether the English name is referencing the _Venus flytrap_ or _venom._

- _Dur_ is general the word for "flower."

**_Rugdurnáj_** is proposed with the meaning "Strange-flower frog." The \<k> from _ruk_ assumes the voicing of the following \<d> in _dur_.
## 004. Charmander

Originally named ヒトカゲ meaning "fire lizard." The translated name refers to "char" or the marks of burning, and the salamander, especially in its mythical role as a spirit of fire.

- _Gif_ refers to fire and flames.
- _Burmelas_ is the word for cooking things (not just food), _Anburme_ being the participle "cooked"
- _Dompe_ is the word for burning (for light).
- _Paci_ is the word for black, which might also be used to describe the appearance of charring?
- _Qés_ is a word for hot, and would include uncomfortable heat
- _Gir_ is a word for hot that primarily refers to edible or potable heat (good heat for a meal or hot drink)
- _Èngif_ is a construction that could mean something like "aflame" (cf. _ènla_ "in-law, by law, under law")
- _Dojìn_ is a newly coined word meaning "lizard."

Looking over the options above, we've decided to propose using _domjin_, a contraction of _dompedojìn_ "torch-lizard" as the core element of the name for this family.

**_Dudomjìn_** is proposed with the meaning of "small-torch-lizard" or "candle-lizard."

## 005. Charmeleon

Originally named リザード, the name was simply a phoneticization of "Lizard." The English name references the chameleon, a type of lizard.

**_Dompojìn_** is proposed with the meaning of "torch-lizard."

## 006. Charizard

Originally named リザードン, often romanized as "Lizardon." *Don* is thought to suggest its size or mass, or perhaps being a boss. In this stage, it grows wings:

- **Deham** is the word for a wing.

**_Dedomjìn_** is proposed with the meaning _winged torch-lizard_.
## 007. Squirtle

Originally named ゼニガメ _zenigame_, meaning "pond turtle." The english name uses _squirt_ or shooting water or liquid, as a reference to a squirt gun, and because the word _squirt_ rhymes with the _turt-_ of _turtle_.

**_Morgambrya_** is proposed with the meaning "small lake-turtle."

**_Ganchonrya_** is proposed with the meaning of "swift sea-turtle."

## 023. Ekans

Originally called アーボ (usually romanized: "ābo" or "arbo"), the name is actually "boa" spelled backwards. This is preserved by the English name, as Ekans is simply a reversal of the letters in "snake." It is proposed that the Adinjo Journalist name similarly be  a reversed form of their word for either snake in general, or a specific type of snake.

## 024. Arbok

Originally called アーボック, this is a phonetic reversal of the word "cobra," which is them phoneticized into Japanese. The English form, Arbok, preserves this while stylistically choosing to use \<K> instead of \<C>.

## 025. Pikachu

Originally called ピカチュウ, this iconic monster's name is more or less preserved across all localizations, to the best extent it can be.

**_Píkachu_** is the canonical form of this name in Adinjo Journalist.
